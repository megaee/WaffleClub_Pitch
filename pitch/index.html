Presenter notes · Presenter 1 (~70 sec)

WaffleClub works like Nespresso, but for waffles. You get a smart waffle iron for one euro, and the real product is the batter: every month you receive pre-portioned pouches. You scan the pouch, the iron sets the right heat and time. You add milk, shake, and bake. No measuring, no failed batches. There are three plans: Single for 9.99, Family for 19.99 — our most popular — and a Party Box on demand. Flavours rotate every month, including vegan, gluten-free and savoury options. Why does it work? Customers get a cheap start, no waste and variety. The company gets recurring revenue, data on what people like, and loyalty, because the mixes are made for the iron. Our first task: customer journeys. We built three personas who reach WaffleClub through completely different channels, and followed each of them through five stages: awareness, consideration, acquisition, service and loyalty.

Presenter notes · Task 1 – Target groups & personas (~30 sec)

We have three target groups, and one persona for each. One: busy families with kids. Our persona is Lisa, 38. She sees us on Instagram and takes the Family plan. Two: students and young singles on a budget. Our persona is Leo, 22. He joins through a friend's referral link and takes the Single plan. Three: gift buyers. Our persona is Tom, 45. He isn't a user himself; he buys a gift box for Christmas. Each of them has one key moment where we win or lose them.

Presenter notes · Journey 1 – Lisa (~40 sec)

Lisa scrolls Instagram in the evening and sees a Reel of kids making waffles, with the hook 'your waffle iron for one euro'. Consideration: on the product page she filters for gluten-free because of her son, and asks the AI chat whether the iron is safe for kids. Presenter notes · Journey 2 – Jonas (~40 sec)

Presenter notes · KPIs & analytics (~40 sec)

We measure one KPI for every stage of our customer journey, tracked with Google Analytics 4 and our shop dashboard. Awareness: the click rate on our Instagram and Google ads, target 1.5 percent or more. Consideration: the bounce rate on the shop, below 45 percent. Acquisition: checkout conversion of at least 3 percent, and a customer acquisition cost below 25 euros per subscriber. Service: at least 90 percent of new customers bake their first box in the first week. That was Lisa's moment of truth. Loyalty is the most important stage for a subscription: monthly churn below 6 percent, a customer lifetime value of at least 150 euros, and one in five customers refers a friend. Our north star KPI ties it together: how many subscribers are still active after three months.
